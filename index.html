         Currently, I am doing a Research and Teaching Assistant in the LAMA at Université Paris-Est Créteil. I defended my thesis on June 19th and I am currently looking for a postdoc position. I did my PhD under the supervision of Stéphane Seuret and Stéphane Jaffard on Inhomogeneous or anisotropic function spaces: modelling and estimation of multifractal spectra at Université Paris-Est Créteil.
         I am working on the identification of the generic regularity and multifractal properties verified in some Besov-type spaces presenting inhomogeneity or anisotropic features. I consider the regularity properties of functions and measures in function spaces with controlled oscillations presenting fractal properties. I also worked on the characterisation of the anisotropy in anisotropic Besov-type spaces, which are function spaces of huge interest in fluid mechanics and PDEs.
    I am also interested in the numerical estimation of regularity features on mathematical functions, signals and textures, such as the estimation of multifractal properties of extensions of Riemann’s Fourier series to confront known theoretical results and numerical estimations, as well as provide numerical evidence to conjecture more precise results in the case of partial theoretical results.
         My research interests are multifractal analysis, functional analysis, harmonic analysis, geometric measure theory, genericity (Baire and prevalence), stochastic processes, and signal processing.

    Publication

      Preprint

    1. Q. Rible and S. Seuret. “Traces of functions in Besov spaces in Gibbs environment”. Submitted. 2026. (abs,pdf) ▼ Abstract: This paper focuses on the traces of functions in inhomogeneous Besov spaces $B^{\xi}_{p,q}$, where $\xi$ is a product of capacities defined as powers of Gibbs measures. Inhomogeneous Besov spaces have been introduced and studied by Barral-Seuret in 2023 to solve the so-called Frisch-Parisi conjecture. First, we show that the traces of functions in $B^{\xi}_{p,q}$ always belong to a specific inhomogeneous Besov space, and find an upper bound for the singularity spectrum of all traces of all functions in inhomogeneous Besov spaces ${B}^{\xi}_{\infty,q}$. Then, we determine the singularity spectrum of function traces for a prevalent set of functions in ${B}^{\xi}_{\infty,q}$, proving in particular that the traces do not all share the same regularity properties.
    2. Q. Rible and S. Seuret. “A non-vanishing property for tensor products of wavelets”. Submitted. 2026. (abs,pdf) ▼ Abstract: We prove that, given a wavelet $\psi$, it is possible to choose some multi-integers $(p_j=(p_{j,1},...,p_{j,d}))_{j \in \mathbb{Z}} \in \mathbb{Z}^d$ such that, for every $x=(x_1,...,x_d) \in \mathbb{R}^d$, for infinitely many integers $j$, the tensorized wavelet $\prod_{i=1}^d \psi(2^j x_i-p_{j,i})$ does not vanish at $x$. This non-vanishing property is essential for analysing some generic regularity properties in certain Sobolev and Besov spaces. The proof relies on an assumption regarding the zeros of $\psi$, which we numerically verify for the first Daubechies wavelets.
    3. Q. Rible. “Prevalent smoothness in inhomogeneous Besov spaces”. Journal of Mathematical Analysis and Applications, 566(2):131093, 2027. (doi, abs,pdf) ▼ Abstract: We consider inhomogeneous Besov spaces recently introduced by Barral-Seuret in 2023. When $\mu$ is a capacity, we prove that prevalent elements in such spaces are multifractal (with a singularity spectrum that we determine), completing previous Baire generic results already obtained.

      Thesis

    4. Q. Rible. “Inhomogeneous or anisotropic function spaces: modelling and estimation of multifractal spectra”. PhD thesis. Université Paris-Est Créteil, 2026. (abs,pdf) ▼ Abstract: This thesis consists of three parts. The first investigates certain inhomogeneous Besov spaces. The second studies regularity properties of functions in anisotropic Besov-type spaces. The third adopts a numerical perspective and focuses on the properties of Riemann’s ``non-differentiable'' series. The first part focuses on the regularity of traces of functions belonging to inhomogeneous function spaces recently introduced by Barral-Seuret. These spaces generalise classical Besov spaces and depend on an ``environment'' $\xi$ (which should be thought of as a probability measure) and two positive real parameters $p$ and $q$. The environment $\xi$ introduces a constraint into the definition, allowing functions to display different behaviours in different regions of $\mathbb{R}^D$. A first result establishes an equivalence between these spaces and spaces defined by a Sobolev-Slobodeckij-type formula depending on $\xi$, reinforcing that these new spaces are genuine analogues of classical Besov spaces. Under natural hypotheses on $\xi$, we then identified the prevalent multifractal behaviour of functions in these spaces. Multifractal analysis consists of determining the pointwise Hölder exponent of a function around each point, and estimating the Hausdorff dimension of the sets of points having the same exponent. Introduced essentially by Frisch and Parisi in a seminal 1984 paper on turbulence, multifractality is now recognised as an important property in analysis, probability theory and dynamical systems, among others. It is customary to prove properties on prevalent sets of functions, a notion from functional analysis formalising the idea of ``almost every function'' in infinite-dimensional spaces. While prevalent multifractal behaviour was already known for classical Besov spaces, the corresponding Hausdorff dimensions are significantly easier to compute in that setting. This thesis also studies traces of functions in inhomogeneous Besov spaces, meaning the restriction of a function defined on $\mathbb{R}^D$ to $d$-dimensional subspaces. This question naturally arises in physics, where experimental measurements are only one-dimensional traces of phenomena such as the three-dimensional turbulent flows. In contrast to standard Besov spaces, the regularity of the traces in inhomogeneous Besov spaces may strongly depend on the region where the restriction is taken. The techniques rely on measure theory, functional analysis, wavelet methods, and some elements of dynamical systems.
           The second part studies the regularity properties of functions in anisotropic settings. Anisotropy, which arises when multidimensional physical properties vary with direction, appears in many applications such as hydrology and medical imaging. We show that functions in anisotropic Besov-type spaces exhibit maximal regularity with respect to a specific anisotropy structure, which therefore provides an optimal framework for their analysis.
           The third part presents numerical results described as ``experimental mathematics''. The goal is to approximate the Legendre spectrum of certain functions to conjecture their singularity spectrum. The Legendre spectrum is known to provide an upper bound for the singularity spectrum, and for large classes of functions and stochastic processes, coincides with it. We applied this technique to several variants and extensions of Riemann’s non-differentiable function, which recently attracted renewed attention due to their unexpected appearance in turbulence modelling in recent work of Banica-Vega. Recent results provide upper and lower bounds for the singularity spectrum that do not coincide, and numerical evidence may help determine what to expect between these values.

      Poster

    5. S. Jaffard, Q. Rible, and G. Saës. Multifractal Analysis of the Riemann’s Series. Harmonic and Multifractal Analyses : from Mathematics to Quantitative Neuroscience. 2023. (abs,pdf) ▼ Abstract: In this poster, we consider a slightly generalised type of function defined from Riemann's series. After introducing the tools used, we compare theoretical results already established with numerical results to assert the accuracy of the numerical analysis. This provides a basis for conjecturing results for the generalised Riemann Fourier series.
